# Accuracy scorecard: unified detector on the frozen benchmark

Generated by `python scripts/detect.py --evaluate` on all 200 benchmark documents. The answer key was verified against the frozen hash. To regenerate this page, rerun that command and copy `data/processed/detect/scorecard.md` below the "Generated scorecard" heading.

## What the numbers say

1. **Fabricated citations: 273 of 273 caught, with 0 false flags.** This holds for statutes, court rules, cases and wrong-reporter citations. Because none were missed, the bootstrap interval collapses to a point. The honest lower bound is about 98.9% (1 − 3/273). The 100% result depends on the corpus. Any real authority outside the corpus would be flagged as fabricated. Two known instances (AS 11.56.807 and .810 in doc-0136) are excluded, as the data dictionary requires.
2. **Altered quotes: 61% recall, 67% precision.** The average hides a split:
   - **Planted alterations: 40 of 40 caught.**
   - **Natural misquotes: 9 of 40 caught.** All 31 quote misses are natural misquotes. 28 of them were never extracted as a passage at all, so the quote checker never saw them. The remaining 3 were extracted but judged acceptable.
   - By alteration type: added words are caught 97% of the time, mixed edits only 12% (n = 26). Mixed edits are mostly natural paraphrase-style misquotes.
3. **All 24 false quote flags are unquoted (copied) passages, and none are quoted ones.** Copied-passage detection is the main source of false alarms. Counting only high-severity quote flags raises precision to 84%.
4. **Document risk: every one of the 103 corrupt documents is flagged (risk ≥ medium), with 83.7% precision.** There are 20 "false alarm" clean documents, and each one was flagged for a single altered copied passage:
   - 9 of them contain a labeled natural misquote, so the flag is correct under the "any labeled error" target.
   - The other 11 have no labeled error. Quote labels are only a sample, so some of these may be real, unlabeled changes. They need a manual review before they are called false positives.
5. **The perfect AUC is an artifact of how the benchmark was built.** 102 of the 103 corrupt documents had two or more fabricated citations planted, so a threshold of 0.84 would separate clean from corrupt perfectly. We deliberately do not use it, because it would call a real document with one fake citation "medium". The thresholds are a fixed policy instead (see [detector.md](detector.md)).
6. **Extraction recall is 92.3% under the strict matching rule.** Most misses (197 of 270) are the extractor capturing a *more specific* subsection than the key, such as `AS 25.24.160(a)(4)` where the key has `AS 25.24.160(a)`. Existence scoring still matches those, which is why missed extractions cost no fabricated-citation recall.
7. **Speed and reliability:** 20 ms median and 53 ms p95 per document, plus about 0.2 s one-time corpus load. 0 errors on 200 documents. That is fast enough to check an upload on the fly.

## Where to improve (ranked by harm to users)

1. **Natural misquotes, which are paraphrases presented as the law.** Recall is 22.5%, and most are never extracted. Extend passage extraction beyond quotation marks and verbatim runs, for example with sentence-level semantic similarity to the cited section.
2. **False alarms on copied passages.** These cause all 24 false quote flags and all 20 document false alarms. Review the 11 unlabeled cases first. If they are genuine false positives, require high severity before an unquoted passage raises the document's risk.
3. **Out-of-corpus real law.** Before deploying beyond the benchmark corpus, add an "unknown authority" state (look the authority up in a fuller statute source) so that real law is not shown to users as fabricated.

## Caveats

- Document-level separation is easier on this benchmark than in real use: 102 of 103 corrupt documents had two or more fabricated citations planted, so a perfect AUC mostly reflects the planting recipe. The risk thresholds are a policy (one fabricated citation = high risk), not fitted to this benchmark.
- The detector was developed on these 200 documents; there is no untouched test set, so all numbers are in-sample. The even/odd stability table is a sanity check, not a held-out evaluation.
- Fabricated-citation existence is checked against a fixed corpus of Alaska family-law authorities. Real law outside the corpus would be flagged as fabricated (two known cases are excluded from scoring).
- Quote labels are a sample: many passages have no answer-key row, so quote precision counts every unlabeled flagged passage as a false positive and may understate it.
- Bootstrap intervals collapse to a single point at 0% or 100%. With n items and none missed, the one-sided 95% lower bound is about 1 - 3/n (273 fabricated citations: about 98.9%).
- Clean documents can contain natural misquotes; some document 'false alarms' under the corrupt target are correct catches under the any_error target.

## Counting policies

- Unique fabricated authority per document; unextracted fabrications count as misses.
- Unique authority per document.
- Isolates the existence check from extraction misses.
- Positive = extracted quote containing an answer-key altered_text; all other extracted quotes are negatives. Planted and natural alterations both count.
- Positive = extracted quote containing an answer-key altered_text; all other extracted quotes are negatives.
- Unit = document. Positive = target label true. A document is flagged when its risk level is 'medium' or higher; unreadable documents score 1.0.
- Ranking quality of the score, independent of thresholds.
- Unique normalized (type, citation) per document; whitespace/case normalized; case signals and pinpoint pages ignored; civil/evidence rule aliases normalized; bare rules remain distinct; subsections and lists/ranges remain exact.
- Wall time of Detector.check, corpus already loaded.
- Share of documents where any stage raised.
